# RelocateMath: U.S. relocation planning guide

[Open the RelocateMath calculator](https://relocatemath.com/)

RelocateMath helps people compare a U.S. interstate move using modeled take-home pay, housing and other costs. This public guide is maintained by the creator of RelocateMath and links to the live tools.

## Choose a tool

| Planning question | Tool | How to use the estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Would a move improve my monthly budget? | [State-to-state relocation calculator](https://relocatemath.com/) | Compare places, then enter a salary offer, housing, other monthly expenses and a moving budget. |
| How do destination cities compare? | [City cost-of-living comparisons](https://relocatemath.com/cost-of-living/) | Use local estimates as a starting point, then replace them with actual housing quotes. |
| What should I budget for the move itself? | [Moving-cost calculator](https://relocatemath.com/moving-cost/) | Explore moving options and obtain carrier quotes before making commitments. |
| What income might support a move? | [Salary-needed calculator](https://relocatemath.com/salary-needed/) | Compare the modeled lifestyle benchmark with your own expenses. |
| I live and work in different states. Where do I start? | [Interstate commuting tax calculator](https://relocatemath.com/commute/) | Compare full-year wage estimates, then check withholding, resident credits, local taxes and remote-work rules with official forms. |
| Where do I buy coverage after moving? | [State Marketplace insurance guides](https://relocatemath.com/health-insurance/) | Follow the official marketplace for enrollment and eligibility; distinguish historical premium averages from upcoming coverage. |
| How could retirement taxes differ? | [Retirement tax comparison](https://relocatemath.com/retire/) | Explore the modeled income assumptions and confirm individual treatment with a qualified adviser. |

## A practical comparison workflow

1. Choose the current state and destination, and enter salary and household settings.
2. Review the tax and housing assumptions before interpreting the result.
3. Enable the personal budget and enter the destination salary offer, monthly housing and other spending on both sides.
4. Add a one-time moving budget. Include deposits, closing costs and lost income if they apply to your comparison.
5. Compare ongoing monthly change with the first 12 months after moving costs.
6. Save or print the report and revisit it when actual quotes or salary offers change.

For example, if a move improves the ongoing budget by $500 a month and costs $6,000 up front, the first-year improvement is $0 and recovery takes 12 months, assuming unchanged monthly costs.

## Understand the limits

A difference after selected costs is not the same as total savings. Missing food, transport, insurance, child care, debt payments or payroll benefits can materially change a personal budget. Typical housing costs are estimates, and a full-year comparison does not prepare a part-year tax return.

Read the [methodology and data sources](https://relocatemath.com/methodology/) for coverage, exclusions and uncertainty. Calculators are planning estimates, not individual tax, legal or financial advice.

## Moving-year taxes and coverage

Use the [moving-year tax checklist](https://relocatemath.com/guides/taxes-the-year-you-move/) to organize residence dates, work locations, wage records and withholding. Annual income cannot safely be split only by days before and after a move. The full-year calculators do not prepare part-year returns.

Marketplace enrollment information is labeled by coverage year. Historical cost comparisons remain labeled with their data year. A move may create a Special Enrollment Period; use the official marketplace's eligibility process or the [HealthCare.gov screener](https://www.healthcare.gov/screener/) for federal coverage. A link or estimate does not establish eligibility or complete enrollment.
